What this means for the detector dataset

  • Every file shares one format (2400×1080, yuv420p, H.264 Main, AAC 48 kHz stereo, median — Mbit/s), so no per-file preprocessing is needed beyond decoding.
  • Frame rate swings from about — to — fps inside one file, with — freezes longer than 0.5 s. Frame indices are not a clock: sample, split and track by timestamp, and expect a tracker to see uneven time steps.
  • Players are small: the median on-pitch person box is — px tall and the ball about — px across at 1080p. Train at high input size (1280 to 1920) and keep small-object augmentation gentle.
  • The pretrained COCO model finds — on-pitch people per live frame on average, against 22 players plus officials on the pitch, and sees the ball in only —% of live frames. The ball is the class that most needs labelled data.
  • Sharpness splits the recordings into two groups: — measure a median Laplacian variance of — in live play, the rest about —. That points to a different in-game graphics or recording setting on those days; check that the val and test matches cover both groups.
  • Replays, cutscenes and menus take —% of the time. The score-panel test separates them cleanly, so keep filtering them out before sampling.
